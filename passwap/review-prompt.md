# Adversarial review request: passwap

You are reviewing a deployed, immutable Solana program and its machine-checked proof. Your job is to break it, or to show precisely why it cannot be broken. Do not summarize the design back to us, do not praise it, and do not soften findings. We would rather read ten wrong attacks than miss one right one.

## The design in one paragraph

A maker deposits token A into a vault and records a rate (at least `amount_b` of token B per `amount_a`; 0 means free), a `not_before` time, and a *claim key*: an Ed25519 public key derived client-side from a passphrase by PBKDF2-SHA512, 600,000 rounds, salt `"passwap/v1/" || offer_address`. The vault is the associated token account of a PDA that is an SPL token multisig, threshold 2 of `[offer PDA, maker, claim key]`. A take (signed by the claim key and a payer) takes any `take_a` up to the vault balance and pays `pay_b` with `pay_b * amount_a >= amount_b * take_a`; `pay_b = 0` is admitted only for a free offer and then no token-B accounts are touched. A take that empties the vault closes vault and offer; otherwise the offer stays open. Cancel (maker, after `not_before`) refunds the vault balance. Fees: a flat 1000 DREGG at make from the maker's DREGG account; 0.088% of `pay_b` at take; both to a fixed recipient's associated accounts. Anyone may transfer more token A into a vault; the program bounds takes by the vault balance, not the record.

## Attack surfaces, in the order we care

1. **The specification.** Are the theorems the right theorems? Look for vacuous or unreachable preconditions, `assume`, `admit`, `external_body`, `#[verifier::external]`, trusted `broadcast` lemmas, and any postcondition that is weaker than the prose in `PROPERTIES.md`. Check that `*_conditions` actually constrains every account the plan later names, and that completeness (`None ==> !conditions`) is not made true by an unsatisfiable `conditions`.
2. **The core/adapter gap.** The core trusts the facts. Construct a transaction where the adapter builds facts that satisfy `take_conditions` or `make_conditions` while the runtime state differs from what the facts claim: duplicated account keys with different flags (`by_key` returns the first match), the same account in two roles (maker == payer, `maker_dregg == maker_ata_a`, `payer_ata_b == maker_ata_b`), accounts owned by the wrong program with plausible bytes, a multisig account whose head parses but whose tail or owner is wrong, PDA seeds, `create_program_address` versus `find_program_address`, lamport pre-funding of the offer or multisig PDA before creation, `close_offer`'s lamport arithmetic and the closed-account revival pattern, CPI signer privilege escalation, and compute or stack limits reachable by a hostile caller.
3. **Token-program semantics the proof assumes but does not model.** The custody claim rests on SPL multisig checking `m` of `n` signers. Attack it through Token-2022 extensions: permanent delegate, transfer hooks (extra accounts on `transfer_checked`), transfer fees (the vault or the maker receives less than the plan's amount), confidential transfers, non-transferable mints, default account state frozen, freeze authority on either mint, CPI guard, mint close authority, interest-bearing amounts. For each, state whether a maker, a claimer, the mint authority, or a third party gains something, and whether the recovery path survives.
4. **Economics and griefing.** Front-running a claim (the claim key must sign, but can a transaction be replayed or reordered?), partial-fill manipulation, the rate check at the u64 and u128 boundaries, rounding of `priceFor` versus `clears_rate`, fee dodging, fee-account closure by the recipient as a denial of service, rent held hostage, offers that can never close, dust, and `not_before` versus the cluster clock.
5. **The passphrase and the client.** Offline guessing against the public claim key (state the cost), normalization pitfalls, the salt, the exported claim keypair, and everything the page loads: the pages fetch `@solana/web3.js` and `tweetnacl` from unpkg with no subresource integrity, run on GitHub Pages, and default to a third-party public RPC. Say what a compromised CDN, host, or RPC can and cannot do to a user (steal funds, mislead amounts, mislead decimals, forge confirmations).
6. **Recovery.** The parties can move a vault with raw token instructions. Show a state in which they cannot, or in which one party alone can.
7. **Anything we have not listed.** This item matters most.
